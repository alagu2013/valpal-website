Every business is different. Here are a few things worth knowing before starting the conversation.

Have another question?
What is a business valuation, and why does it matter?

A business valuation estimates the economic value of a company at a specific date. In an M&A transaction, it provides a reasoned basis for negotiations by examining cash flows, market evidence, assets, and risk—not just the asking price.

How do you determine the value of a company?

The approach depends on the business and the purpose of the engagement. Common methods include discounted cash flow, comparable company or transaction analysis, and asset-based valuation. Assumptions and sensitivity analysis help explain the resulting value range.

What is the difference between valuation and due diligence?

Valuation considers what a business may be worth. Financial due diligence examines the quality of earnings, working capital, debt, and other financial risks behind that value. Together, they support better-informed transaction decisions.

What information is needed to get started?

Typically, recent financial statements, management accounts, forecasts, details of debt and assets, and context on the proposed transaction. The exact scope and information requirements are agreed before work begins.

How long does a valuation engagement take?

Timing depends on complexity, the agreed scope, and the availability and quality of information. A realistic timetable should be established after the initial discussion rather than promised before the business has been reviewed.
